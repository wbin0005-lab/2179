Understanding How Demographic Shapes Macroeconomic

Australia's taxation system is increasingly unsustainable due to demographic shifts. Historically, the workforce supported a small retiree population, resembling a wide-bottomed pyramid (▲). However, as birth rates decline and life expectancy rises, the demographic profile has transformed into a narrow, top-heavy bullet shape (🚀). This change reduces the tax base, leading to inefficiencies in funding public services as fewer workers bear the financial burden for an expanding aging population. The current tax structure, rooted in an outdated model, struggles to adapt to these demographic realities.

Ultimately, visualization visually proves that while Australia's demographics have shifted from a stable pyramid to an inverted bullet, our tax structure remains anchored to an old framework—placing an unsustainable weight on a shrinking workforce.

Datasource: Population Pyramids - PopulationPyramid.net

Widening Gap of Wealth in Age Distribution

The chart tracks household net worth distribution across age groups from 2002 to 2025, revealing a widening generational wealth divide in Australia. Australians aged 65 and over see their share of total wealth increase from about 20% in 2002 to over 32% by 2025, while those under 34 hold a minimal and stagnant wealth slice. This disparity is exacerbated by older individuals being less affected by interest rate hikes, as they often own their homes outright and face fewer financial barriers. Consequently, wealth accumulation is concentrated among older demographics, placing a greater tax burden on the diminishing working-age population.

Housing Market Analysis

Understanding House Tenure

When analyzing the connection between migration and wealth in Australia, it becomes evident that the introduction of working-age taxpayers has contributed to a shrinking tax base. Older generations dominate capital and real estate, while younger people and new migrants struggle in a tight rental market or accumulate significant debt. The Reserve Bank of Australia's interest rate hikes disproportionately affect those with heavy mortgages, as increasingly fewer people own homes outright. This creates a structural inequality where older cohorts benefit from monetary policy, while younger workers and migrants bear its consequences.

Understanding Rent and NOM correlation

The visualization highlights a direct correlation between Net Overseas Migration (NOM) and Rent Consumer Price Index (CPI) growth in Australia. During late 2023 and early 2024, NOM peaked at approximately 560,000, coinciding with a severe rental inflation rate of 7.5% to 7.8%, primarily driven by an overwhelming influx of people relative to limited housing availability.

However, as the government curtailed migration numbers to around 310,000 in 2024 and 2025, the rental market pressure diminished significantly, resulting in a drop of rent inflation to 3.5% by April 2026. This situation underscores the financial struggles faced by younger generations and migrants trapped in a high-demand rental market, possessing minimal national wealth.

Furthermore, it critiques the Reserve Bank of Australia's (RBA) reliance on high interest rates as a remedy for inflation, arguing that the actual cause of rental inflation was not consumer spending but the imbalance between population growth and housing supply. The eventual decrease in migration volumes, rather than interest rate hikes, was the primary factor that alleviated rental inflation by 2026.

The RBA Cash Rate Journey

In terms of conventional economic theory, the main mechanism for combating high levels of inflation lies in increasing the cash rate. By deliberately making borrowing expensive, the RBA attempts to dampen aggregate demand by businesses and consumers and encourage saving, which should result in the return of inflation towards its desired (2-3%) range.

However, as shown in the visualization, after the fast period of consecutive increases, the curve became flat and stuck in a high level regime until 2026. Such persistence in keeping rates high was caused by the high level of "stickiness" of the inflation rate. The domestic economy has had difficulties dealing with capacity constraints, a tight labor market, and supply chain shocks, leading to high prices.

Key Insight: Sticky inflation forced the RBA to maintain high cash rates until 2026. Impacted People: People with mortages

Understanding Inflation Peaks

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks the cost of everyday goods. The green shaded area represents the RBA's target band of 2% to 3%.

As shown in visualization, while inflation stayed mostly stable within this target band for decades, the dramatic post-COVID surge completely shattered it, peaking at a massive 6.6%. Prompting RBA to raise cash rate to cool down local consumer spending (in previous chart).

Key Insight: Notice the massive spike outside of this band following 2020. This was driven by global supply chain issues and domestic stimulus, It aggressively forces local mortgage holders to cut back, even though the root causes of the inflation are happening globally, completely out of their control.

Discretionary

The chart analyzed reveals the limitations of conventional monetary policy in addressing inflation, particularly through interest rate hikes. It breaks down the Consumer Price Index into Discretionary (optional) and Non-Discretionary (essential) spending, highlighting that the recent inflation surge (2021-2022) is driven largely by Non-Discretionary essentials, like food and rent, due to supply chain issues and energy crises.

By 2026, Discretionary spending has significantly decreased, indicating consumers can no longer cut back on essentials. The reliance on interest rate increases, which suppress Discretionary spending, fails to alleviate the burden of essential costs, inflicting further pain on households. Additionally, the economic context is compounded by population changes leading to housing shortages, driving up rent inflation.

Ultimately, the chart illustrates that traditional interest rate strategies are ineffective in mitigating inflation linked to necessary expenditures for survival.

Key Insight: Conventional interest rate hikes are a blunt instrument failing to curb inflation because the problem is driven by survival, not luxury
