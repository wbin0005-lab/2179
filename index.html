Understanding How Demographic Shapes Macroeconomic

Australia's taxation system is increasingly unsustainable due to demographic shifts. Historically, the workforce supported a small retiree population, resembling a wide-bottomed pyramid (▲). However, as birth rates decline and life expectancy rises, the demographic profile has transformed into a narrow, top-heavy bullet shape (🚀).

This structural change reduces the taxable labor base, leading to severe inefficiencies in funding public services as fewer workers bear the financial burden for an expanding aging population. Ultimately, our analysis visually proves that while Australia's demographics have fundamentally shifted, our tax structure remains anchored to an old framework—placing an unsustainable weight on a shrinking workforce.

Datasource: Population Pyramids - PopulationPyramid.net

Widening Gap of Wealth in Age Distribution

The chart tracks household net worth distribution across age groups from 2002 to 2025, revealing a widening generational wealth divide in Australia. Australians aged 65 and over saw their share of total wealth surge from 20% in 2002 to over 32% by 2025, while those under 34 hold a minimal and stagnant wealth slice.

This disparity is exacerbated because older individuals are heavily insulated from interest rate hikes, as they often own their homes outright and benefit from high yields on savings. Consequently, wealth accumulation concentrates at the top, leaving younger demographics with the burden of adjusting to economic tightening.

Connecting these two pieces reveals the loop driving Australia's structural tension. The high concentration of wealth in older generations highlights a problem policymakers aimed to solve in the 1990s: an aging population paying less tax, alongside a shrinking local workforce. The net migration strategy served as an economic quick-fix, aggressively importing young, skilled taxpayers to artificially balance the dependency ratio.

However, this massive influx created a structural demand shock that completely outpaced housing supply. This intense competition for shelter immediately transferred windfall wealth back to older property owners, meaning the migration strategy inadvertently worsened the generational wealth divide by inflating asset values and trapping young workers in crushing rental debt loops.

Housing Market Analysis

Understanding House Tenure

When analyzing the connection between migration and wealth in Australia, it becomes evident that housing tenure acts as a vector for financial inequality. While older cohorts dominate capital and real estate, younger families and new arrivals are increasingly excluded from outright ownership.

Because of this divide, RBA interest rate hikes disproportionately penalize mortgage-holders and renters. Outright owners are left unscathed, meaning monetary tightening acts as an unequal mechanism that squeezes the disposable income of working-age Australians while leaving mature wealth untouched.

Understanding Rent and NOM correlation

The visualization highlights a direct structural correlation between Net Overseas Migration (NOM) and Rent CPI growth. During late 2023, NOM peaked at historical highs, directly sparking a severe rental inflation rate of 7.5% to 7.8% driven by an overwhelming supply imbalance.

Crucially, as migration volumes were curtailed toward 310,000 across 2024 and 2025, rental market velocity cooled rapidly, bringing rent inflation down to 3.5% by April 2026. This tight correlation strongly critiques the RBA's heavy reliance on macro rate hikes.

The core data indicates that rental inflation was solved by adjusting structural population inflows, not by squeezing consumer demand through interest rates. Rate hikes did little to change housing supply but heavily penalized households caught in the crossfire.

The RBA Cash Rate Journey

In terms of conventional economic theory, the main mechanism for combating high levels of inflation lies in increasing the cash rate. By deliberately making borrowing expensive, the RBA attempts to dampen aggregate demand by businesses and consumers and encourage saving, which should result in the return of inflation towards its desired (2-3%) range.

However, as shown in the visualization, after the fast period of consecutive increases, the curve became flat and stuck in a high level regime until 2026. Such persistence in keeping rates high was caused by the high level of "stickiness" of the inflation rate. The domestic economy has had difficulties dealing with capacity constraints, a tight labor market, and supply chain shocks, leading to high prices.

Key Insight: Sticky inflation forced the RBA to maintain high cash rates until 2026. Impacted People: People with mortgages

Understanding Inflation Peaks

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks the cost of everyday goods. The green shaded area represents the RBA's target band of 2% to 3%.

As shown in visualization, while inflation stayed mostly stable within this target band for decades, the dramatic post-COVID surge completely shattered it, peaking at a massive 6.6%. Prompting RBA to raise cash rate to cool down local consumer spending (in previous chart).

Key Insight: Notice the massive spike outside of this band following 2020. This was driven by global supply chain issues and domestic stimulus. It aggressively forces local mortgage holders to cut back, even though the root causes of the inflation are happening globally, completely out of their control.

Discretionary

The chart analyzed reveals the limitations of conventional monetary policy in addressing inflation, particularly through interest rate hikes. It breaks down the Consumer Price Index into Discretionary (optional) and Non-Discretionary (essential) spending, highlighting that the recent inflation surge (2021-2022) is driven largely by Non-Discretionary essentials, like food and rent, due to supply chain issues and energy crises.

By 2026, Discretionary spending has significantly decreased, indicating consumers can no longer cut back on essentials. The reliance on interest rate increases, which suppress Discretionary spending, fails to alleviate the burden of essential costs, inflicting further pain on households. Additionally, the economic context is compounded by population changes leading to housing shortages, driving up rent inflation.

Ultimately, the chart illustrates that traditional interest rate strategies are ineffective in mitigating inflation linked to necessary expenditures for survival.

Key Insight: Conventional interest rate hikes are a blunt instrument failing to curb inflation because the problem is driven by survival, not luxury
